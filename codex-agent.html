How ChatGPT Codex Is Used to Grade the Assignment

After students submit the AI interaction, Codex uses an `Agents.md` instruction file to grade the transcript. The agent is strict: it rewards learning evidence and applies caps when the student only uses AI to produce polished answers.

The agent's role

Codex grades the learning process, not the AI's performance.

The agent reads the transcript and asks who is doing the intellectual work. If the student sets goals, asks questions, tests AI output, revises ideas, and applies later learning, the evidence is stronger. If AI controls the entire lesson, the score must remain low.

01

Read the full interaction

The agent must not grade from isolated excerpts or the final essay alone.

02

Find who leads

It checks who asks meaningful questions, introduces angles, evaluates claims, and moves the dialogue forward.

03

Check developmental plausibility

It looks for a believable movement from partial understanding to stronger reasoning.

04

Apply hard caps

Passive AI use, no critique, no learning transfer, or front-loaded sophistication limits the score.

05

Write transparent feedback

Each mark is justified with evidence from the interaction, strengths, and areas for improvement.

False positives

The agent is designed not to be fooled by polish.

AI-led conversation

If AI structures every stage and the student mainly responds, the total score normally stays low.

No student-led inquiry

If the student does not ask at least two substantive self-initiated questions, prompting and direction are capped.

No critical evaluation

If the student never questions or refines AI output, critical reflection receives little credit.

Front-loaded sophistication

If advanced language appears too early without visible buildup, the work cannot receive a high mark.

Output from the agent

The feedback is structured and evidence-based.

The agent reports the total score, criterion-by-criterion marks, objective attainment, strengths, areas for improvement, and a final evaluative comment. This makes the grading transparent while keeping the focus on the student's learning behavior.
