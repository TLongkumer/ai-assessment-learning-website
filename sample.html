Step 2

A Dedicated Sample Shows What Good AI Use Looks Like

The sample assignment demonstrates the expected pattern: the student sets a learning goal, answers the AI, asks their own questions, pushes back, applies concepts to cases, accepts critique, and finally asks for a synthesis based on the conversation.

Good sample pattern Goal -> question -> example -> critique -> revision -> synthesis

Sample topic

Deontological ethics in Google AI Studio

The sample transcript is not presented as a model to copy word-for-word. It is a model of learning behavior: how a student can use AI to understand a theory, test it with real cases, and gradually produce stronger reasoning.

Opening

The student refuses instant outsourcing

The first prompt asks AI not to write the answer immediately. The student names goals: understand duty, moral law, universalizability, respect for persons, and strengths and weaknesses.

Early learning

The student asks sharper questions

After giving an initial understanding, the student asks how deontology differs from utilitarianism and how moral duty differs from obedience to authority.

Testing

Examples become analytical tools

Cheating, hostel safety, and environmental regulation cases are used to test universalizability, motive, respect for persons, and conflicting duties.

Critical turn

The student pushes back

The student notices that deontology could be misunderstood as blind rule-following and asks for critique of their own reasoning.

Transfer

The student explains what changed

Later, the student says their understanding moved from "follow the rule" to rational moral testing of maxims, dignity, and duties.

What makes this sample strong

The sample is useful because the learning is visible.

Student-led prompting

Questions come from the student, not only from the AI tutor.

Concrete cases

The theory is tested against college life, public safety, and public policy examples.

Revision after feedback

The student tightens a judgment after the tutor points out a weakness.

Final synthesis is earned

The essay comes after the conversation has built enough conceptual material.

Sample transcript highlights

Assessment lesson

A sample is not just an example answer. It is a sample learning path.

This is why the website separates the sample from the rubric. The sample helps students see the expected behavior; the rubric explains how that behavior is judged.
