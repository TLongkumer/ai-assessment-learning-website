How I Assess Learning With AI

Students work in Google AI Studio using a teacher-designed system prompt. They follow a clear instruction sheet, interact with AI as a learning partner, submit the conversation as evidence, and are assessed with a rubric that rewards agency, questioning, critical reflection, and learning transfer.

See the student workflow See how it is graded

The simple version

The final essay is not the main evidence. The AI conversation is.

A polished final answer can hide passive AI use. This assessment asks students to show the learning path: what they asked, what they misunderstood, how they corrected themselves, how they tested examples, and how they later used AI feedback in their own reasoning.

1

Create the tutor prompt

The teacher writes a system prompt that makes AI guide, question, and delay final answer production.

2

Give student instructions

Students set up Google AI Studio, enter the system prompt, and begin with a learning-focused opening.

3

Students learn with AI

They ask follow-up questions, request clarification, test cases, and revise earlier understanding.

4

Submit the process

The transcript, notes, and final synthesis are submitted so the learning path can be evaluated.

5

Grade with Codex

A strict grading agent checks student agency, progression, critique, examples, and rubric alignment.

The grading philosophy

Reward visible learning, not hidden automation.

Strong evidence: the student asks meaningful questions, challenges AI output, revises ideas, and applies feedback later.

Weak evidence: the student gives polished paragraphs, lets AI control every stage, and submits a strong final answer without process.

Central test: who is doing the intellectual work, the student or the AI?
